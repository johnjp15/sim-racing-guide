# 03: Chassis, cockpits, mounting, seats, motion and tactile

Research pass: 2026-10-09. US market, USD unless stated. All prices and dimensions checked 2026-10 against the linked page. "Conf" = confidence (H/M/L). Independent of the old `research/` folder.

## Key takeaways

1. Spend order for a beginner: solid mounting first, load cell brake second, tactile third, motion last (or never). A reviewer's "top 3 upgrades" list is exactly rig, load cell, haptics (https://ocracing.com/guides/my-top-3-sim-racing-hardware-upgrades/).
2. A desk clamp is fine for 2 to 5 Nm wheels and potentiometer pedals. The real desk problems are the chair rolling and pedals sliding, both fixable for almost nothing.
3. Foldables cost $179 to $300 (NLR GTLite Air $179, GTLite Pro $299, Playseat Challenge X $299.99). Maker claims go to 13 Nm, reviewers see flex above about 8 Nm or with a hard load cell brake.
4. Entry aluminum profile ("8020") rigs now start around $349 to $540 without a seat (Trak Racer TR40S $349, RigMetal Basic $409, TR80S $499, RigMetal Plus $539, ASR 3 $549.99). That is the same money as a mid tubular steel cockpit, so profile is the default "buy once" answer if the rig can stay set up.
5. Budget for the extras: seat ($229 to $449), seat brackets and slider ($40 to $90), monitor mount ($129 to $400), shipping. A "$499 rig" is realistically $900 to $1,100 sat in and driving.
6. 4080 profile is enough for essentially any beginner wheelbase (Trak Racer rates its 4080 TR80S to 16 Nm). 40120 and 40160 matter for 20 Nm+ bases, very stiff pedals, or motion actuators. Bolt-up quality and bracing matter as much as profile size.
7. Seat: a reclining sim seat ($229 to $299) or a junkyard car seat ($20 to $55) suits most beginners better than a fixed fiberglass bucket. Buckets are size sensitive and less comfortable for long sessions.
8. Tactile is the best immersion per dollar: one Dayton BST-1 is $59.98 plus a small amp and SimHub (license from 8 EUR). ButtKicker Gamer Plus is a $279.95 all-in-one. It makes you no faster.
9. Motion got cheaper in 2026 (Moza HMA150, 4 actuators, $2,999) but is still an immersion luxury that needs a rigid profile rig, floor space and tolerant neighbors. Not a beginner purchase.
10. Biggest avoidable mistakes: buying a foldable for a 12 Nm+ wheelbase, forgetting monitor mounting and seat cost, buying a bucket seat unseen, and buying motion before a rigid rig and a load cell.

## 2. Profile sizes: what 4080 / 40120 / 40160 means

- The number is the cross-section in mm. 4080 = 40 x 80 mm, 40120 = 40 x 120 mm, 40160 = 40 x 160 mm. "8020" is a US brand name that became the generic term; "15 series" is the inch equivalent (1.5 x 3 inch on the ASR 3: https://www.advancedsimracing.com/products/asr-3).
- Taller section = much stiffer in bending along the tall axis. That is why uprights and base rails get the big profile.
- Trak Racer's own chart is the cleanest maker statement: 40x40 base = 10 Nm, 80x40 = 16 Nm, 120x40 and 160x40 = 30 Nm+ (https://trakracer.com/products/tr160-v5).
- Stiffness is a system property: SimXPro notes "profile size, bracket quality, and smart mounting decisions change everything" (https://simxpro.com/blogs/guides/what-is-an-8020-sim-rig-the-aluminium-profile-explained-in-plain-english). A loosely built 40160 flexes more than a square, tight 4080.
- Owner tip repeated on r/simracing: if a new rig feels wobbly, loosen everything, square it on a flat floor, re-tighten in order (https://lr.vern.cc/r/simracing/comments/1cufcgg/has_anyone_upgraded_from_a_4080_profile_rig_to_a/).

### Why flex matters

- Force feedback is information. If the wheel deck moves, some of the detail goes into bending the rig instead of your hands.
- Brake flex is worse than wheel flex: a load cell reads force, so a pedal plate or seat that gives changes how the same leg push reads, and consistency suffers (https://ocracing.com/guides/my-top-3-sim-racing-hardware-upgrades/).
- Flex also means noise (creaks, clunks) and bolts that walk loose.

### When rigidity stops mattering

- For a 5 to 12 Nm wheelbase and pedals up to about 60 to 100 kg brake force, a properly built 4080 rig is effectively "done" (maker rating 16 Nm; multiple owner reports). Conf M.
- 40120 / 40160 is worth it for: 20 Nm+ bases, hydraulic or 150 kg+ pedals, motion actuators lifting the frame by its corners, or very heavy/tall drivers.
- The price gap is small (TR80S $499 vs TR160 V5 $730), so "overbuy slightly" is defensible, but it is not a performance upgrade for a beginner.
- No controlled deflection test comparing the three profile sizes was found. Treat all Nm ceilings as guidance.

## 3. Desk use: problems and cheap fixes

No prices quoted here because none were verified this pass. All items are generic hardware store or household items.

| Problem | Cause | Cheap fix |
|---|---|---|
| Chair rolls back under braking | Casters | Caster stopper cups, swap casters for fixed glides, lock casters, or put rear casters in a pair of old shoes |
| Chair swivels | Gas lift bearing | Non-swivel chair (dining chair), or a strap from chair base to desk leg |
| Chair tilts back | Tilt mechanism | Lock tilt; if no lock, use a fixed chair |
| Pedals slide | Smooth floor, light pedal base | Push pedals against the wall, a board between pedals and wall, non-slip rug pad, or screw pedals to a plywood board that the chair sits on (your weight anchors it) |
| Pedals tip up | High brake force on a short base | The plywood board fix, or a heel plate |
| Desk shakes / monitor wobbles | Light desk, cantilevered top | Lower FFB strength, push desk against wall, add a diagonal brace, move monitor to a wall or separate stand |
| Clamp slips or dents the desk | Small clamp pad, thick or bevelled edge | Wood shim between clamp and desk underside, check max clamp thickness in the wheel's manual |
| Setup/teardown fatigue | Everything loose | A wheel stand solves this before a cockpit does |

- Desk limits (judgment, Conf M): 2 to 5 Nm bases are comfortable on any solid desk. 8 Nm works on a heavy desk with strength turned down. Above that, the desk is the weak link and bolt-down mounting is the right answer.
- Glass desks, hollow-core (honeycomb) tops and standing desks at height are the worst cases.
- A load cell brake on a rolling office chair is the classic bad combination: fix the chair first.

- Buckets: body shape matters most; try in person or read fit-specific reviews (https://ocracing.com/guides/sim-racing-seats-explained/). No hip-width sizing chart verified this pass.
- Recliners suit long sessions and mixed use; buckets suit shorter intense sessions (same source).
- Warning from a Trak Racer guide: on the TR120 range the GT fiberglass seat back "can physically pop out of its mounting" under heavy braking; a rally-style seat is recommended for load cell pedals (https://simracingcockpit.gg/trak-racer-buyers-guide/). Conf M, single source.
- GT position: upright-ish, knees bent, pedals low. Works for everything and for VR. Formula position: hips low, feet near hip height, reclined. Only worth it if you drive open-wheel almost exclusively. Hybrid rigs (NLR F-GT $599, F-GT Lite $299) convert, but converting is tedious (https://nextlevelracing.com/racing-cockpits/ ; https://ocracing.com/?p=50149).
- Junkyard tips from forums: passenger seats have less wear; prefer manual (non-powered) seats; Miata, 90s Honda, Impreza seats are popular; keep the factory sliders (https://www.gtplanet.net/forum/threads/i-need-a-new-carseat-shopping-at-the-yunkyard.303006). Airbag seats: disconnect and do not power the airbag connector.

## 6. Living-space realities

- Footprint: a profile rig is about 1.35 to 1.45 m long by 0.58 to 0.68 m wide before the monitor stand (Sim-Lab 1350 x 580 mm: https://sim-lab.us/products/gt1-evo-simracing-cockpit ; Trak Racer 660 x 1445 mm: https://trakracer.com/products/tr160-v5). Add roughly 0.5 m behind for seat travel and getting in, and the monitor stand in front. Plan on about 2.0 x 1.0 m minimum for a single screen (judgment).
- Folded foldable: GTLite Pro 104 x 93 x 30 cm (https://support.nextlevelracing.com/hc/en-us/articles/13017066857871-GTLite-Pro-NLR-S031). That is closet-sized, not under-bed.
- Weight: foldables 11.6 to 14.4 kg; steel cockpits 28 to 51 kg; profile rigs 25 to 42 kg bare, about 60 to 80 kg with seat, wheelbase and pedals (bare figures from tables above; loaded figure is an estimate).
- Shipping: profile rigs arrive in 2 to 3 heavy boxes that may come on different days (RigMetal two packages; ASR three boxes: https://www.advancedsimracing.com/products/asr-3). Trak Racer quotes about $30 shipping per large item, $70 for a fiberglass seat (https://trakracer.com/products/tr80s-racing-simulator ; https://trakracer.com/products/rally-style-fixed-fiberglass-seat-1).
- Assembly: OC Racing lists 3 to 6 hours for an NLR F-GT Elite (https://ocracing.com/?p=50149). Expect 2 to 4 hours for a simple 4080 rig plus an evening of ergonomic tuning (estimate). Foldables: under an hour (estimate).
- Stock: many US listings were sold out or back-ordered when checked (Sim-Lab GT1 Evo and P1X Pro, RigMetal Basic, TR120S due mid Nov 2026, most of NLR's US store). Check stock before planning around a specific model.
- Floor protection: Sim-Lab floor mat $59 (https://sim-lab.us/collections/all); NLR Premium XL floor mat $99 (https://store-us.nextlevelracing.com/collections/all?sort_by=price-ascending). A rubber gym mat or carpet offcut does the same job. Rubber feet come with most rigs.
- Casters: Sim-Lab caster set $30 (https://sim-lab.us/collections/all?page=2); NLR lockable castors $79 (https://store-us.nextlevelracing.com/collections/all?sort_by=price-ascending). Must lock; a rig that rolls under braking defeats the point.
- Apartment noise, loudest first (judgment, Conf M): motion actuators, bass shakers bolted to a frame on a hard floor, direct drive wheel rattles through a hollow desk, pedal thumps, your own voice chat. Fixes: thick rubber mat or isolation pads under the feet, keep shakers on the seat not the frame, turn tactile down at night, avoid casters on bare floor while driving.
- Bass shakers transmit structure-borne vibration to the unit below much more than airborne sound suggests. Downstairs neighbors are the limiting factor, not volume.

Honest value assessment:
- Motion is an immersion upgrade, not a lap-time tool (https://ocracing.com/guides/moza-hma150-motion-platform/).
- Seat movers (NLR V3) move you relative to the wheel and pedals, which some find unnatural; chassis movers and 4-actuator systems keep the whole cockpit rigid relative to you.
- Short-stroke systems (50 mm: DK2, D Force 1, D-BOX 1.5 inch) are about road texture, curbs, ABS and weight transfer cues; long-stroke (150 mm: HMA150, DK6+) gives visible pitch and roll. One reviewer preferred the short-stroke detail: "feel the car, not be thrown around by it" (https://ocracing.com/reviews/circuit-one-d-force-1-review/).

- Hidden costs: a rigid profile rig (4-actuator systems are designed for aluminum profile), freestanding monitor stand or VR, cable slack management, floor loading and noise. Not apartment friendly.
- Belt tensioners: arguably the best braking-feel cue in motion, but need a bucket seat and harness, and cost $1,500+ commercially.
- Wind: fun in VR, cheapest of the three, zero effect on driving.
- Beginner ranking for immersion per dollar: tactile ($100 to $350) far ahead of wind ($200 to $440), far ahead of motion ($3,000+).

## 10. Used market and DIY

Used (guidance, not verified sale data; Conf L):
- Profile rigs hold value because aluminum does not wear out. Rule of thumb seen across the hobby: roughly 60 to 75 percent of new for clean name-brand profile rigs, less for tubular and foldables. No sale data was collected this pass.
- Local pickup only is normal: shipping a rig costs more than the discount.
- Check: all T-nuts and bolts present, wheel deck drilled for your wheelbase, pedal plate slots fit your pedals, profile ends cut square, no stripped threads in tapped ends, brackets not bent.
- Foldables: check hinge/hub teeth for wear, fabric tears, velcro life. Tubular: check welds and bent pedal plates.
- Seats: check slider play, recline mechanism lock, bolster foam. Fiberglass buckets: look for cracks at the mount holes.
- Used Adorama price point for a Playseat Challenge ActiFit: $165 vs $199 new there (https://www.adorama.com/used-playseat-challenge-racing-seat-actifit/p/us2007997).
- Deal watch: Fanatec CSL Cockpit V1.5 was $144.99 ($115.99 with app discount) at Woot in mid 2026, expired (https://slickdeals.net/f/19653633-woot-app-115-99-fanatec-sim-racing-csl-cockpit-v1-5-black-at-woot). Clearance deals can beat used prices.

DIY:
- Wood rig (2x4 and plywood): cheapest rigid option if you own a saw and drill; heavy and not adjustable. Material cost not verified this pass.
- PVC: avoid; it flexes.
- DIY extrusion: buy cut-to-length from an extrusion supplier and copy a known design. Savings vs RigMetal/Trak Racer are now small (see 1d notes).
- Junkyard seat on a bought profile rig is the highest-value DIY step.
- DIY tactile (Dayton + SimHub) is easy and well documented.
- DIY motion (SFX-100 style) exists but is a serious project, not for beginners (https://overtake.gg/threads/sfx100-direct-drive-belt-tensioner.185199).

## 11. Common beginner mistakes and myths

- Myth: "You need a rig to start." A desk clamp with a fixed chair is fine for a 3 to 5 Nm wheel.
- Myth: "13 Nm rated" means flex free at 13 Nm. Maker ceilings are survival ratings; reviewers saw flex on the GTLite Pro above 8 Nm (https://ocracing.com/?p=50149).
- Myth: "40160 makes you faster than 4080." Not at beginner torque levels. Assembly quality matters more.
- Myth: "Bucket seats are better." Better for hold, worse for comfort and fit range (https://ocracing.com/guides/sim-racing-seats-explained/).
- Myth: "Motion makes you faster." It does not (https://ocracing.com/guides/moza-hma150-motion-platform/).
- Mistake: comparing a rig-only price with a seat-included price. Most profile rigs exclude seat, brackets, slider and monitor mount.
- Mistake: buying a foldable, then a 12 Nm+ wheelbase and load cell six months later. If an upgrade is likely, go profile first.
- Mistake: load cell brake on a rolling chair or sliding pedals. Fix the mounting first.
- Mistake: forgetting where the monitor goes. Screen distance and height drive immersion more than the frame does.
- Mistake: integrated monitor mount plus a strong wheelbase plus shakers gives a vibrating screen. Go freestanding or wall.
- Mistake: measuring the rig but not the room: add seat travel, entry space and the monitor stand.
- Mistake: ignoring stock and shipping. Several mainstream US listings were sold out or on a 4 to 6 week wait in 2026-10.
- Mistake: mixing brand ecosystems on tubular rigs (accessories are proprietary). Profile rigs accept anything with a T-nut.
- Mistake: bolting shakers to the frame in an upstairs apartment.
- Mistake: paying new price for early-bird or "list" prices: GT Omega shows a permanent-looking early bird discount on every item (https://www.gtomega.com/collections/cockpits).
