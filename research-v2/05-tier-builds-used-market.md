# 05: Tier builds and used-market buying

Research date: 2026-10-09. US market, USD, pre-tax, pre-shipping unless stated. All prices and specs were checked 2026-10 at the linked page. "est." means my estimate, not a sourced figure. Confidence tags: (high) = read on the manufacturer or retailer page this session, (med) = search-result snippet or third-party article, (low) = estimate or older data.

## Key takeaways

1. Entry direct drive is now cheaper than gear-driven wheels were: Moza R3 Xbox/PC bundle is $339 and Moza R5 PC bundle is $379, versus $299.99 list for a Logitech G29/G920. Tier 0 can be direct drive on PC and Xbox.
2. PlayStation is the expensive platform at every tier. Cheapest PS direct drive bundles are about $600 to $800 (Thrustmaster T598 $599.99, Fanatec GT DD Pro 5 Nm $799.99), so a PS "Tier 1" realistically costs $800 to $1,000, not $500 to $700.
3. Fanatec US prices have risen: CSL DD Ready2Race 5 Nm is $639.99 (was $399.95 in 2023). Moza and Simagic now undercut Fanatec badly on PC.
4. Tier 2 ($1,200 to $1,600) and Tier 3 ($1,800 to $2,200) bands still hold. Tier 3 is tight once you add a seat, monitor mount, shipping and tax: budget $1,900 to $2,400 landed.
5. Tier 4 band ($3,500 to $5,500) holds. A PC triples build lands around $3,500, a VR build around $3,200 (Quest 3 went UP to $599.99 in April 2026).
6. Tier 5 band ($10,000 to $18,000) holds for D-BOX class motion, but a "motion lite" build with Moza HMA150 actuators ($2,999) or NLR Motion Plus ($2,799) now lands at $7,000 to $9,000.
7. F1 Arcade rigs are Vesaro chassis with D-BOX G5 haptic actuators running rFactor 2; the newer rig generation uses Simagic Alpha Evo bases, P1000 pedals and GT Neo wheels. The motion hardware alone retails for $6,200 to $8,250, so the venue rig is Tier 5 motion with Tier 3 controls.
8. Warranties mostly do not follow the gear: Fanatec and Thrustmaster state non-transferable; Simagic needs the original order. Price used gear as "no warranty" unless bought from a manufacturer refurb store.
9. Buy-once items: load cell pedals, an aluminum profile rig, and the wheel rim ecosystem. Throwaway items: desk clamps, foldable seats, tube-frame cockpits, gear or belt wheels.
10. Biggest data gap: sold-listing used prices. eBay and Reddit could not be fetched and the search budget ran out, so most used prices below are estimates anchored to a few confirmed data points.

How to use this in the guide:
- The venue seat-of-pants feel comes from D-BOX actuators. The same 4 actuator, 1.5 in kit costs $6,200 to $8,250 at retail before the rig. No tier below Tier 5 reproduces it; bass shakers (Tier 4) reproduce the vibration but not the heave, pitch and roll.
- The venue steering and pedals are mid-range consumer parts (Simagic Evo class, $399 to $699 base; P1000 pedals $469). A Tier 3 home rig matches or beats the venue's controls.
- Venue rigs are tuned for novices and a bar environment (assists on, short sessions). Expect a home Tier 2 rig to feel heavier and more detailed in the wheel than the venue, with no body motion. (inference, low confidence: no source states the venue force feedback settings)
- Wheel, pedal and screen models for the original 2022 London rigs were not published in any source found.

  - Avoid: Logitech G27 and older (no current console support), any wheel without force feedback, sets missing the power supply.

## Cheapest sensible path through the tiers

Principle: spend once on what never changes (rig, pedals, wheel ecosystem), spend little on what you will outgrow.

1. **Try before buying:** a used G29/G920 at $100 to $150 resells for what you paid. Zero-loss trial.
2. **If buying new at Tier 0, buy a direct drive bundle, not a gear wheel:** Moza R5 bundle $379 (PC) or R3 Xbox bundle $339. Every part bolts to a rig later; a G29 does not upgrade.
3. **Skip Tier 1 unless you truly must fold it away.** A $299 foldable is the one purchase with no upgrade path.
4. **Skip the Tier 2 tube cockpit if you have the floor space.** Go straight to an aluminum profile rig ($390 to $770 plus seat). It is the last rig you buy through Tier 5.
5. **Buy pedals once:** load cell set at $369 to $469 (CRP2, P1000). Pedals outlive two or three wheelbases.
6. **Upgrade the base last and buy it on sale:** 9 to 12 Nm ($329 to $429) is enough for almost everyone; sell the entry base (expect about half back, est.).
7. **Display before torque:** triples or VR change the experience more than going from 12 Nm to 21 Nm.

| Step | Buy | Cost | Running total | Wasted later |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Moza R5 bundle on your desk | $379 | $379 | nothing yet |
| 2 | Sim-Lab GT1 Evo + seat + monitor mount | about $740 | about $1,120 | desk clamp |
| 3 | CRP2 load cell pedals | $369 | about $1,490 | SR-P Lite pedals (resell about $40, est.) |
| 4 | R12 V2 base + KS wheel | $658 | about $2,150 | R5 base and ES wheel (resell about $150 to $200, est.) |
| 5 | Triples + stand, or Quest 3 | $600 to $905 | about $2,750 to $3,050 | nothing |

- Net loss on the whole path: about $150 to $250 in outgrown parts (est.), versus $500 to $900 lost by buying a G29, then a foldable seat, then a tube cockpit.
- Console players: on Xbox the same path works with the R3 Xbox bundle at step 1, but Moza bases above the R3 are not confirmed as officially Xbox supported (see Unverified), so step 4 on Xbox means Fanatec ClubSport DD+ ($1,404.99 bundle), Logitech PRO, or Thrustmaster T598. On PlayStation start with a used G29 or go straight to Fanatec GT DD Pro; there is no cheap direct drive first step.
