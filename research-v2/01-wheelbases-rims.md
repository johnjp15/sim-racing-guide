## Key takeaways

1. Direct drive (DD) is now the default recommendation from about $260 up. Gear and belt wheels only make sense under roughly $250 new, or used. Moza R3 bundle is $259 PC / $319 Xbox [M] (https://www.apexsimracing.com/blogs/sim-racing-blog/moza-racing-wheelbase-buying-guide-r3-to-r25-ultra-compared-2026).
2. Platform decides the shortlist before budget does. PlayStation is the most restrictive: only specific licensed bases work (Logitech RS50/Pro PS versions, Fanatec GT DD Pro and ClubSport DD+, Thrustmaster T598/T248R/T300/T-GT II, Logitech G29/G923 PS).
3. Torque: 5 to 8 Nm is plenty for a beginner; 8 to 12 Nm is the "never need to upgrade" zone for most people; above 15 Nm is headroom, not speed. Fanatec itself says many 8 Nm owners run 6 to 7 Nm (https://www.fanatec.com/au/en/explorer/products/racing-wheels-wheel-bases/is-8-nm-torque-on-a-wheelbase-enough/).
4. You are buying an ecosystem, not a base. Rims, quick releases and console licensing are brand-locked. Budget for the second rim and the pedals before picking the brand.
5. Fanatec cut prices hard in Feb 2026 under Corsair: CSL DD 5 Nm $399.99, 8 Nm $479.99, ClubSport DD $699.99, DD+ $899.99, new Podium DD 25 Nm $1,199.99 [H] (https://www.fanatec.com/us/en/c/wheel-bases).
6. Moza has the cheapest torque per dollar on PC: R9 V3 $329, R12 V2 $429, R16 Ultra $529, R21 Ultra $699, R25 Ultra $899 [H] (https://mozaracing.com/collections/wheel-bases). Moza is still not on PlayStation today; PS5 products (R5S Pro, R16S Ultra) were announced 2026-09-17 with no price or date (https://mozaracing.com/blogs/news/moza-tgs-2026).
7. Logitech RS50 (8 Nm DD) is the easiest multi-console DD: PlayStation base plus Xbox hub covers PC, PS and Xbox. Base $349.99 to $449.99, System $599.99 to $699.99 [H] (https://www.logitechg.com/en-us/products/driving.html).
8. Simucube 3 is out (Sport 15 Nm $1,399, Pro 25 Nm $1,599, Ultimate 35 Nm $3,299) and Simucube 2 is still sold cheaper (Sport $999, Pro $1,299) [H] (https://simucube.com/en-us/wheelbases/). None of this is beginner gear.
9. Used: Logitech G29/G920/G923 is the safe used buy ($100 to $170). Used DD bases are worth it only when clearly under current new prices, which fell a lot in 2026; many used asking prices are now above new.
10. Biggest beginner mistakes: buying torque instead of pedals/mounting, ignoring console licensing, clamping a 12+ Nm base to a wobbly desk, and running FFB at 100% (clipping).

## 1. Force feedback in plain terms

- The game computes the force the front tires put back through the steering column. A motor in the base turns that into torque on the rim.
- What you feel: weight building in a corner, the wheel going light when front tires slip (understeer), a snap when the rear steps out, kerbs and bumps.
- Useful signal is the change in force, not the peak. A good base is fast and smooth, not just strong.
- Clipping: the game asks for more force than the base can give, so everything above the limit feels identical. A stronger base run at a lower percentage keeps detail (https://www.apexsimracing.com/blogs/sim-racing-blog/how-much-torque).
- Extras layered on top: Logitech TRUEFORCE and Fanatec FullForce add audio-derived vibration through the motor; both need per-game support (FullForce still game dependent per reviews: https://www.simracinghub.nl/en/?p=27630).

### Drive types

| Type | How it works | Feel | Typical torque | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gear | Small motor, plastic/helical gears to the shaft | Notchy, rattly, dead zone at center, loud | ~2 to 3 Nm | Logitech G29/G920/G923 (2.3 Nm per Logitech: https://www.logitechg.com/en-us/products/driving.html) |
| Hybrid (belt + gear) | Belt first stage, gear second | Smoother than gear, still some grain | ~3 Nm | Thrustmaster T128, T248, T248R (3.1 Nm: https://traxion.gg/thrustmaster-t248r-review/) |
| Belt | Motor drives shaft through belts | Smooth, slightly damped/muted, can fade when hot | ~4 to 6 Nm | Thrustmaster T300, TX, T-GT II, TS-PC |
| Direct drive | Rim bolts to the motor shaft, nothing in between | Sharpest, quietest, most detail, most torque | 3 to 35 Nm | Everything from Moza R3 to Simucube 3 Ultimate |

- "Direct axial drive" (Thrustmaster T598) is still direct drive, with an axial-flux motor, 5 Nm constant (https://www.techradar.com/gaming/thrustmaster-t598-force-feedback-wheel-review).
- Gear/belt lose detail in the transmission; DD does not (https://simracingsetup.com/product-guides/direct-drive-wheels-explained/).

### Torque (Nm) in practice

- Check whether a number is peak or constant/holding. Moza lists R9/R12/R16 as peak and R21/R25 as sustained (https://mozaracing.com/collections/wheel-bases). Fanatec quotes holding torque. Not directly comparable across brands. [M]

| Torque | What it is like | Fits |
|---|---|---|
| 2 to 3 Nm | Enough to learn car control; kerbs and slides are vague | Kids, casual, used starter |
| 3 to 6 Nm | Clear step up in detail; fine clamped to a desk | Beginners, F1 Arcade converts, console couch setups |
| 8 to 12 Nm | Real-car-ish weight for GT and road cars, with headroom | Most people's "forever" base; needs a solid desk or a stand/rig |
| 15 to 21 Nm | Strong enough to hurt wrists in a crash; needs a rigid cockpit | Enthusiasts with an aluminum profile rig |
| 25 Nm+ | Headroom and fidelity, not lap time | Hobbyists who already know why they want it |

- Fanatec: "for most sim racers, 8 Nm is more than enough"; 5 Nm "might suffice" for beginners (https://www.fanatec.com/au/en/explorer/products/racing-wheels-wheel-bases/is-8-nm-torque-on-a-wheelbase-enough/). Vendor source, but matches general reviewer consensus. [M]
- Moza reseller guidance: R21/R25 need a rig or reinforced desk, not a desk clamp (https://www.apexsimracing.com/blogs/sim-racing-blog/moza-racing-wheelbase-buying-guide-r3-to-r25-ultra-compared-2026).

- Cross-brand reality: on PC any USB rim can physically go on almost any base with the right hub/QR, but it loses through-QR power/data and needs a coiled USB cable. On console, only same-brand licensed rims work.
- Pedals/shifters are less locked in on PC (plug into USB separately). On console they must plug into the base, so they are locked to the brand.
- Lock-in ranking, loosest to tightest (my judgment [L]): VRS/Simucube 2, Simagic, Moza, Asetek, Thrustmaster, Logitech RS, Fanatec.

## 4. Wheel rims

| Shape | Typical diameter | Best for | Avoid for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Round | 300 to 350 mm | Road cars, rally, drift, trucks, anything needing more than 180 degrees of hand travel | Nothing; it is the universal default |
| D-shaped / flat-bottom "GT" round | 300 to 330 mm | GT3/GT4, touring cars, general use | Drifting (flat bottom snags) |
| GT "butterfly" (open top, lots of buttons) | 290 to 320 mm | GT3, prototypes | Rally, drift |
| Formula (rectangular) | 270 to 300 mm | F1, open wheel, prototypes; under about 360 degrees of lock | Road cars, rally, drift, parking a truck |
| Rally/drift (deep dish round, no button clutter) | 320 to 350 mm | Rally, drift, with handbrake | |

Diameters are typical ranges from general knowledge, not checked against each product. [L]

- Smaller rim = same torque feels stronger and quicker. A 280 mm formula rim on a 5 Nm base feels livelier than a 330 mm round on the same base.
- Heavy rims dull weak bases. Keep big leather/metal rims off 3 to 5 Nm bases.
- What friends from F1 Arcade expect: a formula rim. What they should buy first: a round or D-shaped rim, because it works in every car.
- When a second rim is worth it:
  - You have settled into two clearly different disciplines (for example F1 + rally/drift).
  - Your base has a real QR (not legacy Thrustmaster screw collar, not a fixed Logitech G rim).
  - You already have load cell pedals. Pedals beat a second rim for lap time every time.

## 6. Common beginner mistakes and myths

- Myth: more Nm = faster. Reality: torque adds headroom and immersion. Pedals (load cell brake) and seat time add lap time.
- Mistake: maxing FFB strength. Causes clipping and arm fatigue; turn game gain down until heavy corners stop feeling "flat".
- Mistake: buying a base that does not work on your console. "PlayStation compatible" must be on the exact SKU. Fanatec CSL DD, Moza (all current), Simagic, Simucube, T818 do not work on PlayStation.
- Mistake: assuming Xbox support is in the base. For Fanatec, Moza (non-R3) and Logitech RS (PS base) it comes from the rim/hub.
- Mistake: comparing peak vs constant torque across brands as if equal.
- Mistake: 9+ Nm on a flimsy desk or folding stand. The mount flexes, the feedback gets mushy, the clamp walks. Budget a wheel stand or rig above ~8 Nm.
- Mistake: buying a formula rim first because of F1 Arcade. It is miserable in road cars, rally and drift.
- Mistake: buying the cheapest base-only DD and forgetting rim + pedals + mount. A $329 base is a $700 setup.
- Mistake: paying list price for old belt wheels. A T300RS GT at $449.99 or T-GT II at $649.99+ loses to a $399 DD bundle (prices: https://www.thrustmaster.com/en-us/universe/racing/).
- Mistake: paying list for a G29/G920. They sell for $180 to $230 regularly.
- Myth: "Fanatec is dead/unsafe to buy." Outdated as of 2026: owned by Corsair, new products shipping, prices cut, 3-year warranty listed. Residual risk is the proprietary ecosystem, not company survival. [M]
- Myth: "Direct drive is dangerous." Under ~10 Nm it is not; above ~15 Nm set a torque limit, keep thumbs out of the spokes, and let go in a crash.
- Myth: "You need a wheel to see if you like it." A used G29 for ~$120 that resells for ~$120 is the cheapest test.
- Mistake: ignoring software. Moza Pit House, Fanatec app/driver, Simagic SimPro, Logitech G HUB all need firmware updates and a game-specific profile; default settings are rarely good.

## 7. Used market

General rules:
- 2026 price cuts (Fanatec in Feb, Moza V3/Ultra refreshes, Simucube 2 after SC3) pushed new prices below many used asking prices. Always check the current new price first.
- Fair used price heuristic: 55 to 70% of current new street price for DD bases in good condition with PSU and receipt; 40 to 55% for gear/belt bundles. [L, my heuristic]
- Always test in person: full lock-to-lock rotation for grinding, FFB for 15+ minutes (heat fade), every button and paddle, pedal axes in the calibration screen.
- Ask for the power supply (model-specific, expensive to replace), QR parts, clamp, original receipt for warranty transfer (often not transferable).
