## 1. Shifters — H-pattern, sequential, or neither

### Key facts

- Most modern race cars (GT3, F1, prototypes, most rally at top level) use **sequential** shifting (paddles or stick). H-pattern matters for: older/classic cars in sims, road cars, drifting (often sequential + handbrake), trucking (real range-splitter H, usually simplified in sim), and anyone who just enjoys it.
- Many mid-range products are **dual-mode**: one unit does H and sequential (Fanatec ClubSport Shifter SQ, Simagic DS-8X, Thrustmaster TH8A with mode swap).

## 3. Button boxes, dash displays, Stream Deck

### Key facts

- The problem these solve: keyboard functions (pit limiter, brake bias, TC/ABS, wipers, lights, seat adjustment) that a wheel rim's buttons can't cover. Every option below is an alternative to taking a hand off the wheel to find a keyboard.
- **SimHub** (free, simhubdash.com) turns dashboards, overlays, and phone/tablet displays into one system — the zero-dollar starting point. Free overlay packs exist (e.g., benofficial2; iOverlay is the common iRacing alternative). [Index]

## 4. Head tracking (the third display option)

### Key facts

- Head tracking lets you look around the cockpit on a flat screen — the budget alternative to triples or VR, and it pairs with a single monitor/ultrawide.
- Known rig-specific gotcha: **Tobii mounts under the monitor and can be blocked by the wheel on a rig** (assettocorsa.net forum reports). [Index]

**Versus VR/triples framing:** head tracking ≈ $0–170 on top of a screen you own; VR ≈ headset cost + PC headroom; triples ≈ 3 monitors + stand + GPU. It's the cheapest immersion upgrade that exists, and the easiest to resell/undo. Downsides: exaggerated look angles take tuning (OpenTrack curves), and it doesn't add peripheral vision the way triples/VR do.

## 5. Gloves and shoes (small money, real effect)

### Key facts

- The actual problems gloves solve: (1) protecting alcantara/suede rims from sweat and oils, (2) grip on high-torque direct-drive wheels, (3) blister prevention on long stints. Shoes: load-cell brakes reward a consistent thin sole (or socks); thick trainers mute pedal feel.

## 8. Room setup & living with a rig

> Most of this section is qualitative community knowledge; it is labelled as such and contains no prices except where tabled above. The wiki should present it as experience/advice, not fact.

### Footprint and placement (from build/cockpit research)

- A fixed aluminium rig with seat is roughly a **desk-sized footprint that does not fold**; triples on a freestanding stand add width beyond the rig itself. Foldable tier (wheel stands, Playseat-style) exists precisely for shared rooms.
- Casters (Section 6) are the middle path: a fixed rig that can be rolled into a corner or against a wall.

### Folding routines (community consensus — anecdotal)

- Wheel-stand users typically: unplug one USB + power lead, fold, store in closet/under bed. The friction is the routine, not the storage size: if setup takes >5 minutes, people race less. (Frame as anecdote.)
- Foldable cockpits trade rigidity (pedal flex under load-cell brakes) for storability — this is the central Tier 1 compromise in the builds research.

### Partners, family, shared rooms (qualitative)

- The rig is a piece of furniture the whole household lives with; the honest selling points are: casters (it moves), a cover (it disappears visually), and cable tidiness (it doesn't look like a server rack).
- Noise: direct-drive bases are near-silent vs belt/gear whine; the loud parts are usually shifter clunks, pedal bottoming-out, and tactile transducers if fitted. Apartment/thin-wall consideration — label as community consensus.
- F1 Arcade angle for the wiki: for a partner who's sceptical of a rig in the living room, the venue is the no-commitment trial — and the foldable tier is the compromise purchase.

### Maintenance reality

- Firmware/software per brand (each vendor has its own app), periodic bolt checks on aluminium profile rigs, alcantara care (see gloves, Section 5), and dust. Counterpoint to the "set and forget" expectation from console gaming.
