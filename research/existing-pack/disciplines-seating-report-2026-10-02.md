**Date context:** 2 Oct 2026 (read dates below) | **Audience:** US beginners, friends coming from F1 Arcade | **Type:** research notes, not wiki prose. Short factual bullets, tables, sources. Prices: verified where cited; everything else labelled **(label estimate)**. **⚠ = unverified / single weak source.**

## How to read budget tiers (editorial labels — use across all sections)
- **T0 – Controller / desk:** gamepad, or entry gear/belt wheel clamped to desk. Label estimate ~$0–300 total add-on.
- **T1 – Entry direct drive:** e.g. MOZA R5 bundle — verified US $349.99–$399.99, 5.5 Nm, 2-pedal, PC-only (Best Buy / MOZA US, read Oct 2026). Label range for tier: ~$300–600.
- **T2 – Mid:** 8–12 Nm DD, load-cell brake, stable rig/alu profile. Label estimate ~$800–1,800 all-in.
- **T3 – High:** 15 Nm+, triples/high-end VR, load-cell/hydraulic pedals, motion. Label estimate ~$2,000+.
- Torque context (secondary guide): 2–5 Nm entry, 5–8 Nm mid, 8–25 Nm top; "beginner fine with 5–8 Nm" — AutoEvolution equipment guide (index).

## 1. Competitive track racing
**Games:** iRacing, Assetto Corsa Competizione (ACC), Le Mans Ultimate (LMU), Automobilista 2 (AMS2), Gran Turismo 7 (GT7)

### (a) What it is
- Closed-circuit, wheel-to-wheel racing vs AI/online field; lap time + racecraft, licences/ratings in iRacing/ACC/LMU online systems.
- Closest step-up from F1 Arcade: same "quali + race" loop, but sim physics, longer stints, tyre/brake management.
- Sub-flavours in same games: GT3/GT4 (ACC, iRacing), Hypercar/LMP + GT endurance (LMU, iRacing), open-wheel/formula (iRacing, AMS2), touring/stock (AMS2).

### (b) Main games/sims in 2026 — platform availability

|---|---|---|---|---|
| iRacing | ✅ Windows | ❌ | ❌ | "runs exclusively on windows PCs… no current plan for a console version" — iRacing.com (index) |

### (c) Gear that matters and why
- **Load-cell brake = #1 upgrade.** Load-cells measure pressure not travel → repeatable threshold/trail braking; position pedals measure travel (ISRTV, index).
- **Paddles, not shifter:** modern GT3/formula/Hypercar are paddle-shift; H-pattern/clutch unused in those classes.
- **Rim with buttons/rotaries:** brake bias, diff, TC/ABS, engine mode are adjusted mid-corner in GT3/Hypercar/formula — button-poor round rim forces keyboard reaches.
- **Wheelbase:** 5–8 Nm is usable; 8–12 Nm gives headroom so kerb/ABS detail isn't clipped (torque tiers, AutoEvolution). Rotation set low (~540–720°) by the cars themselves — ⚠ typical sim setting, general practice not sourced per-game.
- **Button box:** nice-to-have; duplicates rim controls for endurance (lights, wipers, pit limiter, radio).

### (d) Seating position
- GT3/touring/Hypercar: **GT position** (see §C). Formula cars in iRacing/AMS2: formula recline helps immersion but most home drivers stay in GT position for all track racing (OverTake community, index — ⚠ community norm).

### (e) Display / VR preference
- **Triples** = widest physical FOV 170–210°, best side-by-side awareness; **VR** = 90–130° but true depth; single 16:9 still most-used display in OverTake's 2026 community poll (index).
- VR-supported here: iRacing, ACC (PC), AMS2, LMU (SteamVR listing via Kotaku, index), GT7 via PS VR2 (full game, PS5 only).
- ⚠ VR support parity per title/version changes with patches — check before buying a headset for one game.

### (f) Budget tiers that suit it
- Starts at T0/T1 (GT7+controller on PS5 is the cheapest legit entry). Serious online iRacing/ACC: **T1 minimum sensible, T2 sweet spot** (load-cell matters more than extra Nm). T3 = triples/VR + endurance comfort.

## 2. Rally
**Games:** Assetto Corsa Rally, DiRT Rally 2.0, EA Sports WRC

### (a) What it is
- Point-to-point timed stages vs the clock (not vs cars on track), co-driver pace notes, changing surfaces (gravel/tarmac/snow).
- One mistake ends the run — discipline is car placement + listening, not racecraft vs opponents. Rallycross (in DR2.0/EA WRC) is the circuit-racing exception.

### (b) Main games/sims in 2026 — platform availability
| Game | PC | PS | Xbox | Status Oct 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Assetto Corsa Rally** | ✅ Steam EA | ❌ | ❌ | **Early Access since 13 Nov 2025 (Windows); v0.6 released 10 Sep 2026;** full 1.0 targeted H1 2027 per publisher financial reporting (BoxThisLap, index). Console "will be discussed at a later stage"; VR promised during EA, **not at EA launch** (RaceSimCentral interview, index). ⚠ VR status in v0.6 not confirmed in sources read |
| DiRT Rally 2.0 | ✅ | ✅ PS4 | ✅ XB1 | 2019 game, still the cheap complete package; VR on PC only, no PSVR (OverTake, index) |
| EA Sports WRC | ✅ | ✅ PS5 | ✅ XS X\|S | Official WRC licence; VR = PC SteamVR beta only, no PSVR2 (VirtualUncle, index) |

### (c) Gear that matters and why
- **Analogue handbrake — closest thing to essential.** Used "to initiate rotation, tighten the car's line" in rally/drifting; "less important for circuit racing" (Sim-Lab FAQ, index). A button works, but you lose modulation.
- **Round rim 320–330 mm:** you catch slides hand-over-hand; a formula rim has no top to grab. Rally-preferred size 320/330 in community sources (OverTake, index — ⚠ community).
- **Shifter depends on era:** modern Rally1/2 = sequential stick/paddle; historic (Group B, 70s–90s in DR2.0/AC Rally) = H-pattern + clutch. One sequential stick covers modern; add H later for historics.
- **3 pedals** if running H-pattern historics; 2 pedals fine for modern sequential cars.
- **Wheelbase:** needs fast, free self-steer (catching slides) more than peak torque — mid Nm (5–9) with high rotation speed/slew; ⚠ slew-rate priority is general expert practice, not from a primary source read. Set 900–1080° rotation ⚠ (general practice).

### (d) Seating position
- **Rally position: upright, seat higher than GT**, wheel closer/higher — you work the wheel a lot and need leverage for big corrections (see §C).

### (e) Display / VR preference
- VR is very strong for rally (judging crests/distance, looking to apex) where the title supports it (PC: DR2.0, EA WRC beta; AC Rally VR pending ⚠). Otherwise single/ultrawide is fine — no side traffic to monitor. Triples are wasted width here vs track racing.

### (f) Budget tiers
- **T1 works** (DR2.0 is cheap/old). The discipline-specific spend is the handbrake add-on (~$99 class, see §B), not the base. T2 if AC Rally/historics with H+clutch.

## 3. Drifting
**Games:** Assetto Corsa (drift servers/mods), CarX Drift Racing Online, BeamNG drift

### (a) What it is
- Controlled oversteer: initiate, hold angle on throttle/handbrake, link corners; scored on angle/line/speed in tandem (multiplayer trains/battles).
- Not racing the clock — throttle balance and counter-steer speed are the whole skill.

### (b) Main games/sims in 2026 — platforms
| Game | PC | PS | Xbox | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Assetto Corsa (original) | ✅ (mods) | ✅ PS4 base, **no mods** | ✅ XB1 base, **no mods** | Drift packs/servers, Content Manager, CSP = PC only. "Modding is primarily supported on PC. Console versions have limited or no modding" (TheMadeThing, index) |
| CarX Drift Racing Online | ✅ | ✅ PS4 | ✅ XB1 (+Switch) | PC 2017 → PS4/XB1 2020 → Switch 2021 (Traxion, index). Sequel "Online 2" announced for Steam EA Q4 2025 + consoles later (Traxion, Mar 2025) — ⚠ 2026 release status not verified |
| BeamNG.drive | ✅ | 🔜 PS5 19 Oct 2026 | ❌ announced | See §5; PS5 launch has **no mods** (RacingGames.gg, index) — PC is the drift-mod version |

### (c) Gear that matters and why
- **Round rim, larger (330–350 mm ⚠ community sizing, Driftworks/OverTake):** constant hand-over-hand and letting the rim spin through your palms.
- **Handbrake: essential** to initiate/extend slides (Sim-Lab, §2 source). Analogue strongly preferred.
- **H-pattern + clutch pedal: near-essential** for AC drift cars/CarX tuned cars (clutch-kick technique) — ⚠ technique claim is general drift practice, not cited to a primary source read; treat as expert-consensus.
- **Wheelbase:** must self-steer *fast* — the wheel has to spin itself back during transitions. Weak/slow bases (or high in-game damping) are the classic drift frustration; torque without speed doesn't help. Rotation 900°+.
- Load-cell brake: less critical than track racing (left-foot braking matters, outright threshold precision less).

### (d) Seating
- GT/rally upright-ish; you need arm freedom for big wheel work. Formula recline fights you here.

### (e) Display / VR
- VR or ultrawide helps judge angle/look at exit; CarX players often use chase cam on a single screen (playscore/community, ⚠). Triples uncommon.

### (f) Budget tiers
- **T1 + handbrake + clutch pedal add-on** is the real entry (~T1.5). CarX on console with an entry wheel is the cheapest console route; AC drift = PC required.

## 4. Trucking / hauling
**Games:** Euro Truck Simulator 2 (ETS2), American Truck Simulator (ATS)

### (a) What it is
- Long-haul delivery driving + light business sim: take loads, earn, buy trucks/garages, hire AI drivers (Wikipedia ETS2 description, index).
- No racing: the skill is smooth low-speed manoeuvring, reversing a trailer, route/fuel management. Sessions are long and relaxed.

### (b) Platforms Oct 2026
| Game | PC | PS | Xbox |
|---|---|---|---|
| ETS2 / ATS | ✅ (Steam) | ❌ yet | ❌ yet |
- Console versions **announced Aug 2025** for PS5 + XS X\|S (GameSpot, index). **Still no release date in Oct 2026**; ETS2 was publicly playable on PS5 at Tokyo Game Show Sep 2026 (Univers-Simu, index). ⚠ Any 2026 console date from key-shops (e.g. "31 Dec 2026") is a placeholder, not an announcement.

### (c) Gear that matters and why
- **High rotation, low torque:** set the wheel to ~900° for ETS2/ATS (Steam Community marked answer, index). A weak entry base is fine — forces are gentle; a big-Nm DD is wasted money here.
- **Large round rim:** slow hand-over-hand parking work; formula rims are miserable. MOZA even sells a truck/farming control class — ⚠ product-class claim from MOZA store categories (index).
- **Buttons, lots of them:** lights, wipers, indicators, retarder, engine brake, cruise, cameras — a button box / wheel with many buttons beats reaching for a keyboard. ⚠ control list is the games' known control scheme, not enumerated in a source read.
- **Shifter:** automatics/paddles are fully playable; H-pattern with range-splitter is the enthusiast immersion route (needs clutch pedal). Not required.
- **Handbrake:** no — parking brake is a button.
- Head-tracking/VR or triples/ultrawide matters for mirrors and junction checks (display logic, §A sources).

### (d) Seating
- **Truck position: upright, high**, wheel more tilted toward you like a bus wheel (see §C). Comfort over 2–4 h sessions is the whole game — reclined bucket seats are wrong here; a reclining/road-car-style seat wins.

### (e) Display / VR
- Ultrawide or triples (mirror visibility), or VR/head-tracking for shoulder checks. Frame-rate demands are modest vs racing sims ⚠ (general).

### (f) Budget tiers
- **T0–T1 is honestly enough** (entry wheel, 900°, 2 pedals). Spend goes on comfort: seat, button box, bigger screen — not torque.

## 5. Sandbox / physics play & crashing — BeamNG.drive

### (a) What it is
- Soft-body physics sandbox: every panel/suspension part simulates and deforms in real time; freeroam, scenarios, deliveries, chases, time trials, evolving Career mode (Simulation Daily / RacingGames.gg, index).
- The "game" is experimenting: crash tests, off-road, drift, police chases, mod cars/maps (PC).

### (b) Platforms Oct 2026
- **PC:** Steam Early Access since 29 May 2015; still Early Access, no 1.0 date (RacingGames.gg, index).
- **PS5: launches in Early Access 19 Oct 2026** (announced 24 Sep 2026) with 4-player split-screen, FSR3/PSSR Pro support; **no mods, no VR at launch; price not announced** as of 1 Oct verification (RacingGames.gg, index). At the 2 Oct read date this is *upcoming, not yet playable*.
- **Xbox: nothing announced** ("cannot confirm support for other platforms" — BeamNG FAQ via RacingGames.gg, index).

### (c) Gear that matters
- Almost none, specifically: it's fully playable on controller/keyboard ⚠ (general; controller support confirmed for PS5 listing via Push Square, index). A wheel adds fun for driving scenarios; force feedback tuning is famously fiddly ⚠ community consensus.
- Round rim + handbrake = nice for drift/off-road play; load-cell, button boxes, big torque = irrelevant.

### (d) Seating / (e) Display
- Whatever's comfortable; single screen standard. VR exists on PC via OpenXR ⚠ (SimStaff guide, index — single source). PS5 version: no VR at launch (above).

### (f) Budget tiers
- **T0 (controller) is a legitimate full experience.** This is the cheapest discipline in the whole pack to enjoy properly.

## 6. Driving practice / learning to drive a real car
**Games:** City Car Driving (CCD), City Car Driving 2.0, BeamNG, Assetto Corsa free roam

### (a) What it is
- Low-speed, traffic-law driving: junctions, parking, lane discipline, hazard reactions — the opposite loop to racing.
- **Hard caveat for the wiki:** no game here is driver-ed training or a licence substitute; it's familiarity (controls, observation habits) only. ⚠ No source read evaluates real-world transfer — flag any wiki claim about "learning to drive" efficacy as unverified.

### (b) Games/platforms 2026
| Game | PC | Console | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| City Car Driving (original) | ✅ Windows/Steam | ❌ | Built for this: traffic rules selectable by country (USA/Canada/EU/etc), manual+auto gearboxes, VR Oculus/Vive, TrackIR (Steam store page, index) |
| City Car Driving 2.0 | ✅ EA 16 Jun 2026 | ❌ | SteamDB confirms EA date (index). ⚠ Namu wiki reports launch complaints: no VR, wheel support problems (single wiki source, unverified) — so for wheel practice the *original* is currently the safer recommendation ⚠ |
| BeamNG | ✅ / PS5 19 Oct 2026 | see §5 | Good for slow manoeuvres, clutch bite, hill starts in freeroam ⚠ general practice claim |
| Assetto Corsa free roam | ✅ mods PC only | ❌ mods | Traffic/free-roam mods need PC Content Manager (§3 source) |

### (c) Gear that matters and why
- **900°+ round rim, 3 pedals + H-pattern** if the goal is manual-car familiarity (clutch control is the skill); indicators/lights on buttons or stalks ⚠ (enthusiast add-ons, not sourced).
- Display: triples/VR/head-tracking earn their keep here — mirror and shoulder-check habits are the transferable part. VR exists in original CCD (Steam page).
- Wheelbase torque: minimal. Cheap entry wheel > expensive DD for this use.

### (d) Seating
- Upright road-car (GT/truck-like), pedals low like a road car — practising in a reclined formula tub teaches the wrong posture.

### (e)/(f)
- Single screen fine, VR best for observation habits. **T0–T1 suffices;** do not sell a T2 rig "for learning to drive."

## 7. Traffic weaving / street
**Games:** No Hesi (Assetto Corsa servers), AC Shutoko/traffic mods, Forza Horizon open world

### (a) What it is
- High-speed lane-splitting through dense highway traffic ("cutting up"), scored socially/by server points rather than official racing; night-highway street aesthetic (Tokyo Shuto Expressway in Shutoko Revival Project, SRP).
- The console-friendly translation of the same fantasy is Forza Horizon's open-world traffic, not weaving servers.
- Wiki safety note worth printing: this is a game loop; it must never be presented as real-road behaviour to copy. (Editorial, no source needed.)

## 8. Briefly: oval/NASCAR, karting, open-world cruising

### Oval / NASCAR
- What: sustained high-speed banked-oval pack racing; momentum, drafting, spotter awareness; iRacing (PC) is the sim home; **NASCAR 25** (iRacing Studios) launched PS5/XS 14 Oct 2025, PC 11 Nov 2025, US $59.99 standard (iRacing.com + Speedway Digest price, index).
- Gear: round/larger rim, steady (not fast) wheel work; triples or VR for seeing the pack beside you; 2 pedals + paddles/sequential (stock cars are 5-speed H in reality ⚠ — modern NASCAR sequential; don't overclaim). Seating: GT upright. Tiers: T1+ (console NASCAR 25 on entry wheel is fine).

### Karting sims
- KartKraft (Windows PC only, Wikipedia index), Kart Racing Pro (PC), KartSim for rFactor 2 (PC), plus karts inside AMS2 (PC) and GT7 (PS) (Traxion kart round-up, index).
- Gear: karts are direct-steering, tiny rotation, no gears (except shifter-kart classes) → small rim, 2 pedals, strong *immediate* FFB at low Nm ⚠ (general kart characteristics, not sourced per-title). Seating: low, reclined-ish kart posture is niche — almost nobody should build for this alone.

### Open-world cruising
- Just driving with no objective: FH5/FH6 (§7), BeamNG freeroam (§5), AC freeroam mods (PC), ETS2/ATS as cruising (§4).
- Gear verdict: whatever you own; a round rim + quiet, comfortable upright setup beats any race part. Controller is fine (FH especially).

## B. One rig, multiple disciplines

### What swaps easily (PC)
- **Rim swap via quick release** — the single most valuable multi-discipline feature: formula/GT rim for track, 320–330 round rim for rally/drift/truck. Verified product behaviour: MOZA/Fanatec-class QRs are hot-swappable within their own system (Fanatec QR2 marketing via repost ⚠; MOZA ES wheel QR listed in bundle specs, Apex Sim Racing index).
- **Add a clutch pedal** to a 2-pedal set (modular sets sell single clutch add-ons; MOZA SR-P Lite clutch listed at $42 by a US retailer, Gigaparts index ⚠ single retailer).
- **Bolt-on handbrake / shifters (USB on PC = brand-agnostic):** a discrete USB handbrake/shifter works with any wheel (Thrustmaster TSS is "a discrete USB device… compatible with any racing wheel" — Tom's Hardware, index).
- **Button box:** generic USB, works everywhere on PC ⚠ (general USB-HID practice).

### What is ecosystem-locked
- **Quick releases are brand systems, not a standard:** Fanatec QR2 "shape is therefore not compatible with QR1" and QR2 wheels are Fanatec-system-specific (Fanatec transition text via Carousell repost ⚠ + Tweedot "system-specific… limiting compatibility", index). A MOZA rim does not click onto a Fanatec base (and vice versa) without adapters that typically lose native button/FFB data functions ⚠ (adapter caveat general).
- **Console licensing is the real lock:** a base must be licensed for PlayStation/Xbox. MOZA only *announced* its first PlayStation bases at Tokyo Game Show 17 Sep 2026, **price not yet revealed** (Traxion, index) — until then MOZA PC bases don't work on PS5. Xbox needs an Xbox-licensed rim/base combo (e.g. MOZA R3-for-Xbox class, Wikipedia DD list index). **Pick your platform before your brand.**
- Pedals/shifters/handbrakes: free mix-and-match **on PC via USB**; on console they usually must plug through the licensed base ⚠ (general console practice).

### Compromises a do-everything build makes
- One rim = wrong everywhere a bit: the accepted single-rim compromise is a **300 mm round/D-shape** — "slightly larger than ideal formula (~285), slightly smaller than a standard 320 round" (OverTake community, index).
- GT seating (see §C) is the compromise posture; formula and truck authenticity are both sacrificed.
- A 3-pedal load-cell set + H shifter + sequential + handbrake covers everything but costs ~$230–280 in MOZA-class add-ons alone (verified prices above) and clutters the rig's right side (shifter *and* handbrake compete for the same mount space ⚠ practical note).
- Wheelbase: a mid 8–12 Nm DD with 1080°+ available rotation and fast slew is the one-base answer; you then turn rotation *down* per game.

## C. Seating positions

### The four positions (geometry)
| Position | Seat | Legs / pedals | Wheel | Matches | Long-session comfort |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Formula** | Very reclined, seat base low, back ~ laid-back single-seater posture | Legs raised, feet up near seat height; pedals high and angled toward driver ("reclined, feet-up… legs stretched ahead" — Playseat Formula Instinct retailer description, index) | Low, close, angled up toward chest | F1/open-wheel in iRacing, AMS2, F1 games; Playseat Formula cockpits claim authentic F1 position "co-developed with professional F1 drivers" (Playseat via AVLGEAR, index ⚠ manufacturer claim) | Worst for long sessions for most adults; getting in/out is a manoeuvre; lumbar support essential (NLR manual suggests lumbar cushion use differs F1 vs GT, index) |
| **GT** | Semi-upright bucket, moderate recline (~ community "seat back around 40 degrees" ⚠ OverTake) | Feet lower than hips, pedals below seat level, near-vertical pedal faces | Chest height, arms slightly bent | GT3, touring, Hypercar, most track racing, drifting, weaving, oval — the default | Best all-round; hours-long endurance stints are designed around it |
| **Rally** | Upright, seated higher than GT | Pedals lower, more knee bend for bracing | Higher and closer (leverage for big, fast corrections) | Rally, rallycross, off-road | Good; upright = easy in/out between stages |
| **Truck** | Upright and high, road-car/truck chair height | Pedals low and flatter, like a van | Larger rim, more tilted (column angle) toward driver | ETS2/ATS, driving practice, cruising | **Best for multi-hour sessions** with a proper reclining seat; worst for feeling "race car" |

Evidence note: NLR markets its Elite cockpits for "Formula, GT, Hybrid, Truck, and Tank" positions from one frame (NLR official, verified live) and describes the **hybrid** explicitly as "seat in GT position but pedals in a formula style position" (NLR F-GT page, index) — i.e. the industry itself sells the in-between as a named position.

### Why switching Formula ↔ GT on one rig is hard
To do it properly, **four things must move**: (1) seat base angle/height, (2) pedal tray height + angle (formula = pedals up high; GT = down low), (3) wheel deck height + tilt, (4) monitor height/FOV (your eyes move ~a head-height, so screen and in-game FOV/seat position must follow). Community summary: "you need to change: seat angle, steering wheel position, pedals position, monitor position (if racing on screen)" (OverTake, index).
- **NLR F-GT manual, F1→GT conversion (primary document, index):** remove pedal plate from the pedal-angle bar and remount it on the front plate; install GT support posts both sides to *raise the seat*; then "seat angle may need adjusting; wheel angle may need adjusting; wheel position may need adjusting." That's unbolting and re-bolting — minutes-to-tens-of-minutes with tools ⚠ (time estimate editorial), not a lever flip.
- **Aluminium-profile rigs:** T-slot extrusion is infinitely adjustable *with a hex key* — adjustment exists, but every axis is loosen-slide-tighten; it is a workshop task, not a quick release. NLR includes a ratchet tool with Elite cockpits "allowing for quick adjustments" (retailer listing, index) — "quick" here still means tools-out.
- **Seat sliders** (fore-aft only) solve driver height, not position class. **Pedal risers / angled pedal brackets** can fake formula pedal height on a GT rig ⚠ (generic accessory class, no specific product verified). A **second seat** (formula shell + GT recliner) is possible on profile rigs but doubles cost and storage ⚠.

### Practical advice (for the wiki)
- **Buy GT position as the all-rounder;** add formula flavour with a formula rim, not a formula seat, unless F1/open-wheel is >~80% of your driving ⚠ (editorial threshold).
- Pick the seat by your *primary* discipline: rally/truck/practice want upright; only dedicated formula drivers benefit from recline, and they pay for it in every other discipline (§A rows all assume GT-ish posture except karting).
- If two people share a rig, a seat slider + marked pedal/wheel positions beat any "switchable position" marketing.
