## One Rig, Multiple Disciplines — suggested slug: one-rig-multiple-disciplines
**Page purpose (1 line)**
What swaps easily on a PC rig, what is ecosystem-locked, and what a do-everything build compromises.

**Key facts:**
- Rim swap via quick release is the single most valuable multi-discipline feature: formula/GT rim for track, 320–330 mm round rim for rally/drift/truck
- Add a clutch pedal to modular 2-pedal sets; MOZA SR-P Lite clutch listed $42 by a US retailer (single retailer, index)
- Bolt-on USB handbrake/shifter on PC is brand-agnostic (Thrustmaster TSS is a discrete USB device compatible with any wheel — Tom's Hardware, index); generic USB button box works everywhere on PC (general USB-HID practice)
- QRs are brand systems, not a standard: Fanatec QR2 not compatible with QR1 and is Fanatec-system-specific; a MOZA rim does not click onto a Fanatec base without adapters that typically lose native functions
- Console licensing is the real lock: base must be licensed for PS/Xbox; MOZA first PlayStation bases announced Tokyo Game Show 17 Sep 2026, price not revealed (Traxion, index) — until then MOZA PC bases do not work on PS5
- Pedals/shifters/handbrakes mix freely on PC via USB; on console they usually must plug through the licensed base (general console practice)

**Options/alternatives:**
- Do-everything compromise: 300 mm round/D-shape rim (slightly larger than ideal formula ~285, smaller than 320 round — OverTake community, index)
- GT seating as compromise posture; mid 8–12 Nm DD with 1080°+ rotation available, turned down per game, is the one-base answer
- Full cover (3-pedal load-cell + H + sequential + handbrake) costs ~$230–280 in MOZA-class add-ons alone at verified prices above ($99 + $129 or $149 class) and clutters right-side mounts — shifter and handbrake compete for same space (practical note)
