## Cost

### $1,800–$2,200

<small>Assumes: existing PC/console, display, dedicated space</small>

- With rig shipping and sales tax: up to ~$2,400

## At a glance

| Spec | This tier |
|---|---|
| Mount | Aluminum profile rig, separate seat |
| Wheelbase | 12 Nm direct drive |
| Pedals | Mid load cell, the "buy once" set |
| Screen | The one you own, on a stand behind the wheelbase |
| Space | Rig ~150×70 cm (est). Plan 2.0×1.0 m with a screen and room to get in |
| Build time | 3 to 6 hours, plus an evening of adjusting |

## Target user

| Good fit | Skip if |
|---|---|
| Committed, races weekly or in a league | Under ~2 hours a week |
| Wants to stop upgrading | PlayStation and only Gran Turismo. Tier 2 gear on a better rig is enough |

## What it feels like

- Zero flex. Every angle adjustable
- A brake you can stand on. Set it as firm as you like
- 12 Nm: no clipping in normal use

## Pros / cons

| Pros | Cons |
|---|---|
| Rig and pedals are a 10-year purchase | Industrial look |
| Everything bolts on later: shifter, shakers, motion | Heavy, long assembly |
| Nothing is thrown away at Tier 4 | Many small extras: brackets, T-nuts, slider, bolts |
| | Popular rigs are often back-ordered |

## Next upgrade

- Display: triples or VR (Tier 4)
- Not more torque

## Used-market alternative

| | |
|---|---|
| Look for | Aluminum profile rigs: the best used buy in the hobby. They barely wear and cost a lot to ship, so local pickup is cheap |
| Also | Moza R12 / R16, Simagic Alpha, Fanatec ClubSport DD, Heusinkveld Sprint pedals |
| Check | 40-series profile (30-series is flimsy), all brackets and T-nuts, pedal controller box included |
| Avoid | Wood or PVC rigs priced like aluminum, pedals missing elastomers |
